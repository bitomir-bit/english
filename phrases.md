# English Phrases & Vocabulary

A growing collection of phrases, idioms, and vocabulary for practice.
Each entry: **phrase** — meaning. *Example.*

## Everyday Conversation

**He moved to Stockholm a while ago** — general past event, unspecified time. *She left the company a while ago.*
**I'm heading home now** — currently on the way somewhere. *I'm heading to the office.*
**Have a great weekend / How was your weekend?** — standard social phrases.
**Sounds good. That's settled then.** — confirms a decision. *We'll meet at 9. Sounds good, that's settled.*
**Sorry, I couldn't hear what you** — polite way to ask someone to repeat.
**I'm having a hard time hearing you** — softer version of the above.
**To my delight, the landlord replied today** — "to my delight" = I was very happy when it happened. *To my surprise, he agreed.*
**This is my fight — I can't drag you into this** — "drag someone into" = involve them in something they didn't choose. *Don't drag me into your problems.*
**It didn't work, but it was worth a try** — worth attempting even if it fails. *We lost, but it was worth a try.*
**Exactly / Totally / Exactly right** — casual ways to strongly agree. Alternatives to "yes, that's correct."
**We were just talking about you — speak of the devil** — said when someone appears right after being mentioned.
**We should get going** — it's time to leave. *I need to get going or I'll be late.*
**Hold on, let me check something** — asking for a brief pause.
**I'm on it** — I'm handling it. *The report will be ready by noon — I'm on it.*
**You all right?** — casual British way of asking how someone is.
**I'll catch up with you in a moment** — I'll talk to you very soon. *I'll be right with you.*
**Shall we?** — elegant suggestion to start something together. *We finished the drinks. Shall we?*
**From the bottom of my heart** — very sincerely. *From the bottom of my heart, thank you.*
**I've lost hope up to this second / until now** — expressing that hope finally arrived.
**I get you** — I understand. Casual alternative to *that makes sense.*
**I'll give it a shot** — I'll try. *Let's give it a shot and see what happens.*
**Not particularly** — not especially, soft disagreement. *She didn't look particularly happy about the idea.*
**Do you think it's a coincidence or was it planned?** — questioning whether something happened by chance.
**You got a minute?** — casual way to ask if someone has time to talk.
**Correct me if I'm wrong** — polite hedge before stating an opinion.
**Perhaps she is right** — maybe, possibly (slightly formal).
**That's on me** — I take responsibility. *We missed the deadline — that's on me.*
**Behold the sunset** — look at this (dramatic, old-fashioned). *Behold the sunset, isn't it beautiful?*
**After the breakup, I moved on** — stopped thinking about the past, continued living normally.
**Chuffed** — very pleased, happy (British slang). *He'd be right chuffed you made it.*
**Fire away** — go ahead, I'm ready to listen. *Fire away, I'm ready to hear your ideas.*
**Can you keep a secret?** — asking someone to keep information private.
**Hold that thought** — don't forget what you were about to say; wait a second. *Hold that door* = keep the door open.
**He's slow and steady, but he always gets things done** — consistent and reliable even if not the fastest.
**How am I supposed to know?** — I have no way of knowing that (rhetorical, slightly frustrated).
**I'll let you out** — I'll open the door so you can leave.
**I have the hiccups** — the medical condition of repeated involuntary spasms of the diaphragm. *Sorry, I have the hiccups — give me a second before I start the call.*
**Should I just give you her personal number?** — at a Swedish pharmacy: "personal number" = personnummer, used to access prescriptions. *I'd like to pick up a prescription for my daughter — should I just give you her personal number?*
**What is the cause of this?** — what caused this? / why did this happen? *The conversion rate dropped 20% overnight. What is the cause of this?*
**I was overwhelmed with tasks today** — had too many tasks at once, felt unable to cope. *Three urgent tickets came in right before standup.*
**I managed to finish my workout** — succeeded in finishing despite tiredness or a busy day. *I managed to finish my workout even though I only had 30 minutes.*
**Let's agree on a time and do it on the weekend** — let's schedule a specific time to meet or do something on the weekend. *Saturday morning works for me.*
**I jumped out** — left a call or meeting suddenly / exited quickly. *Sorry I jumped out of the call — my kid needed me.*
**Ćevapčići** — small grilled minced meat sausages, traditional dish from Bosnia & Serbia. *We made ćevapčići at the barbecue — people who'd never tried them absolutely loved them.*

**Time constraints made the project harder** — limited time causing difficulty. *We're working under tight time constraints.*
**This tool is underutilized** — not being used enough, more value could be extracted. *We could get much more out of it.*
**I can't recall off the top of my head** — I don't immediately remember, I'd need to check. *Let me verify and get back to you.*

**Can you elaborate on the budget impact?** — explain something in more detail. *Could you elaborate?*
**What's the magnitude of the problem?** — how big or serious is it? *We need to understand the magnitude before deciding.*
**I'm happy to report that...** — pleased to share good news in a meeting or update. *I'm happy to report that we hit our targets.*
**He wanted redemption after his bad performance** — trying to prove yourself after a past failure. *This quarter is his chance for redemption.*
**Not yet, but I'll get to it today** — I haven't done it yet but will. Natural way to respond to "did you finish X?"

**I don't mean to interrupt, but...** — polite way to break into a conversation. *I don't mean to interrupt, but I have a question.*
**Credit to Anna for organizing this** — give recognition/praise to someone. *Credit to the team for delivering on time.*

**Please loop us in on the final report** — include us in the communication/update. *Loop me in when you hear back.*
**We tailored the offer to the client's needs** — customize to fit specific requirements. *A tailored approach works better than a generic one.*
**Don't worry, the booking is taken care of** — it's already handled, no need to worry.
**Let's not deviate from this strategy** — go in a different direction from the plan. *We can't afford to deviate now.*
**What's the head count for the event?** — how many people will be there? *We need head count by Friday.*
**We agreed, but with one caveat** — with one condition or warning attached. *I'll approve it, with one caveat.*
**We need higher granularity in the sales data** — more detail, broken down further. *Drill down to order level, not just daily totals.*
**Without further ado, let's begin** — let's start now without more delay. Common opening phrase.
**You're exaggerating — it wasn't that bad** — making something sound bigger or worse than it really is. *That's an exaggeration.*
**It slipped my mind** — I temporarily forgot something. *Sorry, it completely slipped my mind.*
**He has skin in the game** — he has personal risk or financial stake in the outcome. *He invested his own money, so he has skin in the game.*

**He's gaslighting me** — making someone doubt their own feelings, memory, or reality. *Stop gaslighting me — I know what I heard.*
**My takeaway was that we need to focus on retention** — the main thing I concluded or learned. *What's your takeaway from the meeting?*

**Zero-sum game** — if one person gains, another loses. *Not all business is a zero-sum game — both sides can win.*
**I get imposter syndrome whenever I present** — feeling like a fraud even when you're competent, fear of being "found out."
**I need to reconcile these numbers** — make figures match across reports. *The data doesn't reconcile with what was sent yesterday.*

**Don't jinx it** — don't bring bad luck by being too confident. *I feel good about the deal but I don't want to jinx it.*
**I'm having trouble comprehending this** — I can't fully understand it. More formal than "I don't get it."
**We need to hit the ground running** — start immediately and with full effort. *There's no time to warm up — we hit the ground running.*
**The underlying problem is communication** — the real, hidden issue beneath the surface. *We need to address the underlying cause.*
**Top-line growth** — increase in revenue/sales (as opposed to bottom-line = profit). *The focus this year is top-line growth.*
**The trailer gives a sneak peek** — a brief early preview of something. *Here's a sneak peek at the new dashboard.*
**What are the highlights?** — brief summary of the most important points. *Give me the highlights from the meeting.*

**Most of the team works in the bullpen** — open office area with many desks. *Hot-desking in a bullpen setup.*
**She is very resilient** — handles stress well and bounces back strong. *Children can be very resilient.*
**Playing devil's advocate** — arguing the opposite side to test or challenge an idea, not because you believe it. *I'm just playing devil's advocate here.*
**I'm just playing along** — going with what someone else is doing/saying. *I knew it was a joke, I was just playing along.*
**She was very vocal about the problem** — spoke openly and clearly about it. *He's very vocal about his opinions.*
**One mistake caused a cascade of issues** — a chain reaction where one thing triggers another. *A cascade of failures.*
**Can you give me a breakdown of the costs?** — a detailed list or explanation of each part. *I need a full cost breakdown.*
**Let's drill down into the data** — look deeper into the details. *Let's drill down by region.*

**Let's truncate the text** — cut it down, shorten it. *Truncate the description to 150 characters.*

**Apologies for being late** — a formal/polite way to say sorry for arriving or responding late. *Apologies for being late — the previous meeting ran over.*
**Edge cases** — unusual or rare scenarios that don't behave like typical cases. *The main flow works, but we haven't tested edge cases — what happens if the user has no order history?*

**Diminishing remaining universe** — as items are selected or used, the pool of remaining options shrinks. *We've sent to 80% of the segment — our CTR will naturally drop from here.*
**Choke point** — a narrow place where flow or progress is restricted or slowed down. *The checkout page is a choke point in our funnel — most drop-offs happen right there.*
**I can't join — I have a meeting clash** — two meetings overlap at the same time. *I can't join the 2pm sync — I have a meeting clash with the weekly review.*

## Idioms & Figurative Language

**Stop bragging about your vacation** — showing off, talking too much about your achievements.
**She really hyped up the movie, but it wasn't that good** — built up excitement that wasn't deserved.
**She's hyping you up before your presentation** — giving you energy and confidence (*you've got this*).
**Don't let me down — I'm counting on you** — don't disappoint me.
**I'm just being humble / keeping it low-key** — not showing off even when you deserve credit.
**I got some recognition, but I'm just keeping it low-key** — not making a big deal of it.
**Sometimes it's good to put your guard down and trust people** — stop being defensive, be open.
**He keeps his guard up around new colleagues** — stays cautious and defensive.
**He has a savior complex — always trying to fix everyone's problems** — feels compelled to rescue others.
**The project had a rocky start, but now everything runs smoothly** — difficult beginning, now working well.
**Don't try to reinvent the wheel** — don't redo what already exists and works.
**My brain just checked out for a second** — I stopped paying attention briefly.
**Some people's behavior online really creeps me out** — makes me feel uncomfortable or uneasy.
**I'm being a mess** — I'm disorganized / not performing well.
**I'm on the verge of losing my patience** — about to lose patience.
**The tides are turning** — the situation is changing (figurative).
**We turned into a dead end** — reached a point with no way forward / no growth.
**It felt like everything was slipping away** — falling apart, going out of control.
**Our team was outflanked because we didn't see it coming** — someone gained an advantage over us.
**If you don't innovate soon, you'll be outflanked by cheaper alternatives** — others will win.
**Light them up** — dominate them. *The team really lit them up in the second half.*
**The crowd lit up when the band came out** — became very excited and energetic.
**We got away with it** — did something wrong but weren't caught or punished.
**We got away for the weekend** — took a short trip or break.
**Measure twice, cut once** — think carefully and check before acting. Don't make hard-to-fix mistakes.
**He bowed to the pressure and agreed to the deal** — gave in, stopped resisting (figurative).
**I'm on my way** — I'm coming, I'll be there soon. *I'll be there in 10 minutes.*
**Orion is one of the easiest constellations to recognize** — example of night sky reference.
**The car almost fell into a ravine** — deep narrow valley with steep sides.

**I would appreciate it if you could help** — polite way to make a request. *I would appreciate it if you could review this before the meeting.*
**Let go of me** — demand to be released from someone's grip. *He grabbed my hand and I shouted, let go of me.*
**Back off** — step away / stop pressuring someone. *Back off, you're too close. You need to back off a little.*
**There is no way to know** — expressing complete uncertainty. *There is no way to know what will happen next.*
**The restaurant caters for weddings** — provides food or services for events/people. *Our platform caters to enterprise clients.*
**Does that bother you?** — does it annoy or upset you? *Does the noise bother you?*
**I'm parched** — I'm very thirsty. More expressive than "I'm thirsty." *After the run I was completely parched.*
**You're pissed off at me** — you're angry at me. Note: ❌ "pissed off me" → ✅ "pissed off at me"
**Let's resurrect this idea** — bring something back after it was dropped or stopped. *We shelved it last year, but let's resurrect it.*
**Spiders really freak me out** — scare or disturb me strongly. *That movie freaked me out.*
**He finally snapped** — suddenly lost emotional control after pressure built up. *After hours of stress, he finally snapped.*
**She gave him the cold shoulder** — deliberately ignored or was unfriendly toward someone. *After the argument, she gave him the cold shoulder.*
**The kids dressed up as superheroes** — wore a costume. *We dressed up for Halloween.*
**The movie was highly anticipated** — expected eagerly by many people. *The launch was highly anticipated by the team.*
**Please reconsider your decision** — think again about something. *I hope you'll reconsider.*
**I've got a lot on my plate** — I'm very busy or overwhelmed. *I can't join today — I've got a lot on my plate.*
**Cheer up — things will get better** — telling someone to feel more positive. *Cheer up, it's not that bad.*
**Let's work like there's no tomorrow** — maximum effort, give everything you have. *We need to push hard today.*
**Not in this forum** — this is not the right place or group to discuss something. *Let's take this offline — not in this forum.*
**Can you do me a favor?** — polite way to ask for something. *Can you do me a favor and send the file?*
**I'm on the mend** — getting better after being sick or hurt. *I was sick last week, but now I'm on the mend.*
**I'm famished** — I'm very hungry. More intense than "I'm hungry." *Let's eat — I'm famished.*
**How could you ambush him like that?** — surprise someone unexpectedly, emotionally or physically. *She ambushed him with the question in front of the team.*
**"For sale: baby shoes, never worn."** — Hemingway's 6-word story implying the baby never survived. *Six words, but you feel everything.*
**A chain is only as strong as its weakest link** — a system's strength depends on its most vulnerable part. *Our pipeline is solid, but if the API feed breaks, everything breaks.*
**I couldn't help but get the jitters** — couldn't avoid feeling nervous; jitters = anxiety before something important. *I couldn't help but get the jitters before the big presentation.*
**Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels** — British crime film (1998); "lock, stock and barrel" = everything, completely. *They acquired the startup lock, stock and barrel — offices, staff, IP, everything.*
**Let's get this ball rolling** — let's start / let's begin doing this. *Everyone's here — let's get this ball rolling.*
**Take it with a pinch of salt** — don't believe something completely; be slightly skeptical. *The vendor's numbers look great, but take them with a pinch of salt.*

## Vocabulary (Single Words)

**pragmatic** — practical and realistic, not emotional or idealistic.
**particularities** — specific details that make something unique.
**flawed** — imperfect, containing errors or weaknesses.
**ingenuity** — the ability to solve problems in smart, creative ways.
**endurance** — the ability to keep going even when something is hard or painful.
**durability** — how long something lasts while staying in good condition.
**prevention** — stopping something bad from happening.
**revival** — bringing something back to life or popularity. *There's a revival of 90s fashion.*
**retrieve** — to get something back, recover it. *The app can retrieve data from the cloud.*
**contemplate** — to think carefully and deeply. *She took a moment to contemplate before speaking.*
**amplify** — to increase volume, intensity, or impact. *Social media can amplify a message to millions.*
**compile** — to gather and organize information into one place. *I compiled everything into one report.*
**terminate** — to end completely, formally. *The company terminated his employment.*
**purge** — to remove or get rid of something completely. *The government tried to purge corruption.*
**premise** — the initial idea or assumption behind an argument. *The experiment proved the premise correct.*
**void** — having no legal power; also a feeling of emptiness. *The contract is void.*
**fatigue** — extreme physical or mental tiredness. *After the marathon, I felt total fatigue.*
**meticulously** — very carefully, paying attention to every detail.
**sufficient** — enough. *That's sufficient.* (Sounds formal; casual: *that'll do.*)
**reluctant** — not willing, hesitant. *I was reluctant to speak in public.*
**halt** — to stop. *The army was ordered to halt the advance.* Also noun: *a sudden halt.*
**hazy** — blurry, foggy, unclear. *The mountains looked hazy in the distance. My memory of that day is hazy.*
**intact** — completely whole, not damaged. *The package arrived intact.*
**attuned** — sensitive to, in sync with. *He's very attuned to people's emotions.*
**uphold / upheld** — to support, maintain, or confirm a principle or decision. *She upheld the company's values.*
**snatch** — to take something quickly, often without permission. *My phone was snatched on the train.*
**toss** — to throw lightly or casually. *He tossed the keys on the table. Toss the salad gently.*
**nagging** — bothering someone by complaining or asking repeatedly.
**inherit** — to receive something from someone, often after they pass away. *She inherited her father's talent.*
**trapped** — stuck somewhere, unable to escape. *He got trapped in the elevator for an hour.*
**behold** — to look at (old-fashioned, dramatic). *Behold the sunset!*

## Common Corrections & Awkward Phrases

**"on weekends" vs "on the weekend"**
- *on weekends* = every weekend in general (*I don't work on weekends*)
- *on the weekend* = this specific upcoming or last weekend

**"haul up" vs "hold up"**
- *They hauled him up from the pit* = pulled him out (literal)
- *The manager hauled him up in the meeting* = strongly confronted him (figurative)

**"light up" vs "lit up"**
- *Light them up* = turn on / dominate
- *The crowd lit up* = became excited (past tense)

**"vitals"** — key health metrics (heart rate, blood pressure, temperature). In business: key KPI numbers.

**"rusty" not "rusted"** — when you're out of practice, say *rusty*, not *rusted*. *My English might be rusty.* ❌ *My English might be rusted.*
**"colonel"** — pronounced "kernel" (not "col-o-nel"). *Her father is a colonel in the army.*
**"pissed off at" not "pissed off me"** — ✅ *You're pissed off at me.* ❌ *You pissed off me.* (Unless you mean "you annoyed me" — then: *You pissed me off.*)

**"look up"** — 4 different meanings:
- *look up [something]* = search for information. *I need to look up this word in the dictionary.*
- *looking up* = improving. *Her health is looking up after the treatment.*
- *look [someone] up* = visit or contact after a long time. *I'll look you up when I'm in your city.*
- *look up to [someone]* = admire. *She looked up to her parents for their wisdom.*
